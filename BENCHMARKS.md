## Variant-level analysis (what each engine can and cannot do)

### Image — detects 4 of 4 tamper types
- **Splice** (textured region pasted in): detected (risk 45). Signal: elevated % pixels with ELA > 1.5.
- **Heavy re-compression** (q55→q70 chain): detected (risk 45). Signal: JPEG blockiness ratio ~2.1 vs ~1.0 single-pass.
- **Global contrast/brightness filter**: detected (risk 85). Signal: elevated mean ELA + very high modified-pixel share.
- **Textured overlay**: detected (risk 45). Signal: localized high-density compression zone.
- No false positives on single-compression synthetic photos.

### Video — detects temporal segment anomalies only
- **Spliced segment** (half the timeline replaced with high-texture content): detected (risk 71). Signal: frames in the replaced segment score 1.5x+ above the video's own median ELA.
- **Uniform re-encode** (whole video re-compressed): **missed** (risk 13). A uniform re-encode has no segment elevated *relative to its own baseline*. Honest limitation — ELA is relative; a forensic blockiness measure at frame level would be needed.
- **Static micro-overlay** (small box, 8% of frame): **missed** (risk 9). The spatial signal is drowned by content structure (edge-dense patterns also ring at re-encode).
- No false positives on single-encode synthetic streams.

### Audio — detects monotone/synthetic prosody only
- **Monotone TTS-like tone**: detected (risk 60). Signal: flat zero-crossing-rate variation.
- **MP3 re-encode of speech**: **missed** (risk 20). Lossy re-compression is not visible in the prosody features currently used.
- **Truncated short clip**: **missed** (risk 30–42). Correctly flagged as "insufficient data" internally; deliberate not to alarm on legitimate short voice notes.
- No false positives on natural-modulation speech-like audio.

### URL — detects all tested lexical phishing patterns
- All 12 brand-typosquat / keyword-stuffed / bad-TLD URLs flagged (75–98); all 12 known-safe domains at risk 5.

## What this means for the product (honest reading)

1. **Zero false positives** across all engines at threshold 40 — critical for a consumer trust product.
2. **Image and URL engines** are credible enough to ship as-is *for the signals they test*; both still need real-world sample validation (real AI-generated images, live phishing).
3. **Video and audio engines are not yet ready for public accuracy claims** — they only catch a subset of tamper types. Public UI must present these as "indicator" results, not verdicts (Phase 0 of the market-readiness plan).
4. **Known hard gaps to close later**: frame-level blockiness for uniform re-encode detection; real TTS/voice-clone samples; genuine deepfake videos; real camera vs AI image sets.

## Next steps for accuracy work

- Collect 30–50 genuine samples per class (real camera photos, real AI images, known deepfakes, real speech + ElevenLabs clones, live phishing URLs) and rerun.
- Add frame-level blockiness + audio spectral-flatness-of-highband signals to close the re-encode gaps.
- Re-evaluate thresholds when real-world precision/recall data exists.
